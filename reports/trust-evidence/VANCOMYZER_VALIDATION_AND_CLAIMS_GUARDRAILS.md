# Vancomyzer Validation and Claims Guardrails

## Purpose
Define website-wide guardrails for validation, evidence, comparison, and outcome-related language so Vancomyzer remains credible, clinically cautious, and aligned with actual product capabilities.

## Core rule
Do not make a claim that implies validation, superiority, safety benefit, workflow improvement, ROI, or clinical outcome advantage unless that claim is specifically supported by real evidence that can be cited and defended.

## High-risk claim categories

### 1. Validation claims
Avoid implying that Vancomyzer is validated, clinically proven, externally verified, or institutionally endorsed unless that is actually documented.

Examples to avoid:
- validated dosing platform
- clinically validated recommendations
- proven accuracy
- evidence-backed performance
- hospital-validated workflow

Safer patterns:
- designed to support review of vancomycin dosing workflow
- built to emphasize transparency and interpretability
- intended to help users review assumptions, outputs, and limitations
- designed for clinician-readable workflow support

### 2. Superiority claims
Avoid stating or implying that Vancomyzer is better, more accurate, safer, or more effective than competitors unless supported by direct evidence.

Examples to avoid:
- better than VancoCalc
- more accurate than competing tools
- safer recommendations
- superior Bayesian dosing
- best-in-class dosing platform

Safer patterns:
- emphasizes transparency and clinician-readable outputs
- aims to make assumptions and limitations more visible
- focuses on interpretability and workflow clarity
- may be differentiated by explainability and documentation-oriented design

### 3. Clinical outcome claims
Avoid suggesting that Vancomyzer improves patient outcomes, reduces nephrotoxicity, improves stewardship outcomes, or improves safety unless supported.

Examples to avoid:
- improves patient outcomes
- reduces nephrotoxicity
- improves clinical safety
- lowers error rates
- leads to better dosing decisions

Safer patterns:
- supports structured review of dosing workflow
- helps surface assumptions, outputs, and caution notes
- may assist communication and documentation review
- designed to improve interpretability of workflow outputs

### 4. ROI and operational claims
Avoid unsupported claims about time savings, cost savings, staffing efficiency, or hospital ROI.

Examples to avoid:
- saves hospitals money
- reduces pharmacist workload
- accelerates stewardship productivity
- improves efficiency by X%
- delivers ROI

Safer patterns:
- designed to support workflow review and communication
- includes documentation-ready summary concepts
- aims to reduce ambiguity in output interpretation
- may support more consistent review workflows

### 5. Comparison-page claims
Comparison pages must remain fair, educational, and evidence-aware.

Rules:
- Do not misrepresent competitor capabilities.
- Do not frame Vancomyzer as objectively better without proof.
- Do not use adversarial or dismissive language.
- Do compare positioning, transparency emphasis, workflow readability, and educational style where those are observable.

Safer patterns:
- appears to emphasize...
- may differ in...
- Vancomyzer is positioned around...
- this page focuses on transparency, interpretability, and workflow clarity

## Preferred wording patterns

Use more often:
- designed to support
- intended to help
- focused on
- aims to make
- emphasizes
- built around
- clinician-readable
- transparency-oriented
- workflow-friendly
- interpretability-focused
- documentation-ready
- evidence-aware
- caution-oriented

Use less or avoid unless proven:
- validated
- proven
- superior
- safer
- more accurate
- best
- optimized outcomes
- improves outcomes
- reduces harm
- industry-leading
- enterprise-grade performance advantage

## Page-specific guidance

### Homepage
Safe emphasis:
- clarity
- transparency
- interpretability
- workflow usability
- demo-first exploration

Avoid:
- bold performance claims
- outcome claims
- superiority language

### Trust & Evidence page
Safe emphasis:
- what users should review
- visible assumptions
- visible limitations
- evidence-aware language
- caution and interpretability

Avoid:
- implying formal validation beyond reality
- overconfident trust language

### FAQ
Safe emphasis:
- educational explanation
- practical interpretation help
- clinician-readable answers

Avoid:
- turning answers into product claims
- implying certainty where nuance is needed

### About page
Safe emphasis:
- mission
- product philosophy
- clinician-first design
- transparency and workflow support

Avoid:
- brand mythology
- vague “revolutionizing” language
- unsupported claims about impact

### Contact / Institutional page
Safe emphasis:
- evaluation
- workflow review
- documentation-ready examples
- trust-building assets

Avoid:
- ROI claims
- operational savings claims
- implied institutional validation

### Comparison pages
Safe emphasis:
- educational comparisons
- observable positioning differences
- workflow/readability differences
- transparency/explainability framing

Avoid:
- direct superiority claims
- unsupported performance comparisons
- mocking or dismissive competitor framing
